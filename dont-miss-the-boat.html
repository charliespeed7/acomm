↘︎The argument

You've seen this movie twice before.

Hand on heart: were you all in on search in 2003? On mobile in 2010? Almost nobody was. Every platform shift so far was easy to dismiss at exactly the moment it was cheapest to act.

2003

Search looked optional

Google's ad revenue went from $0.4bn to $29bn in eight years. The retailers who treated ranking as plumbing kept the digital shelf for a decade. Most boards funded SEO after the land grab.

2010

Mobile looked niche

3% of web traffic in 2010; more than half of everything ten years later. The window to be early lasted about three years, and most boards spent it commissioning an app as a PR exercise.

E-commerce was slow for a reason

Twenty years to reach a sixth of US retail, because customers had to change: habits, trust, devices. Every previous shift was gated by human adoption. Hold that thought.

↘︎The moat

Agents don't forget.

The rails are live: Instant Checkout inside ChatGPT, Google's UCP with Walmart and Target signed, payment mandates at the FIDO Alliance. You can't be early anymore. You can still be first, and first compounds, because agents keep score.

Trust records

Agents track whose data was accurate, whose orders completed, whose returns were honoured. Ratings are earned transaction by transaction, and late movers start at zero.

Learned defaults

After fifty clean purchases you're the agent's default endpoint. A challenger has to be better, not equal, to displace you.

Model memory

Assistants are trained on the web as it is now. Readable today means baked into the models that steer tomorrow's recommendations.
